From a purely self-interested and systemic perspective, sharing a profitable plan is rarely altruism; it is usually **market creation** or **strategic positioning**.

If humanity is sufficiently selfish, there are several calculated purposes for "sharing":

### 1. Creating the Market Ecosystem (The Funnel Effect)
By revealing enough information to prove the potential of the mine, you convince others that riches exist. This generates demand for *everything else* necessary to exploit it—the instructions (your course), the tools (shovels), labor, infrastructure, etc. You are not selling gold; **you are selling access and dependency.**

### 2. Establishing Credibility and Authority
Sharing a partial plan establishes you as an expert ("I know how this works"). Once people trust your knowledge on one level, they are much more likely to pay for advanced instruction or specialized tools from the same source. You transition from being a secret keeper to being an **indispensable gatekeeper.**

### 3. Deflecting Risk and Blame
If you keep *all* secrets, others may suspect conspiracy or impossibility. Sharing enough details keeps suspicion focused on the method (which you can monetize) rather than focusing solely on withholding the source of wealth itself. It diffuses resistance by making the process seem achievable for the masses—while keeping the most valuable steps reserved for your premium product.

**In short:** The goal is not to help people get rich, but to ensure that *every single step* toward getting rich requires buying something or paying you first.
